The sampler waswas not the policy.

Research & engineering by Sankur KunduPage updated 15 Oct 2026 · technical report public 16 October 2026, arXiv forthcoming

In reinforcement-learning post-training, one engine samples the answers and another computes the gradient, and the maths is only right if both hold the same model. I built both sides from scratch, a C++/CUDA inference engine and a PyTorch trainer, so that every disagreement between them could be seen. The weight sync was silently rounding away most of each update.

Rounding at sync is the gap

Each source switched on alone, measured on the same 64 fresh rollouts after 1, 3, 10 and 30 GRPO updates: mean change in per-token log-probability (nats, log scale).

At the released weights every source is below 0.002 on average and rounding to bf16 is exactly zero, because the released weights are bf16 values. "bf16 rounding" rounds the trainer's weights to bf16; it is not a bf16 engine. One run, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, GSM8K.

fig. 4 — contributions

What this work adds

Most RL stacks measure the engine–trainer gap as one number and correct it in the loss. ratchet controls both sides, so it can say where the gap comes from.

  1. An observable RL loop

    A C++/CUDA engine that returns every sampled token's log-probability, and a trainer in the engine's own weight layout, logging the gap on every step of every run.

  2. A silent failure, and its fix

    Weight sync into an fp16 engine rounds away most of each update. Computing the trainer with the engine's rounded weights, while the optimizer keeps float32 masters, takes the gap from 5.2 to 0.043 nats.

  3. The cause, isolated

    Rounding at sync is 9–22× arithmetic precision and 15–39× the kernels; bf16 would be worse and grows worse, consistent with recent findings that fp16 reduces the mismatch.

  4. Systems for small hardware

    In-place sync 369× faster than a reload, 1.9× faster steps and a 27× faster sampler, on two T4s; GSM8K 48.1% → 52.5%.
