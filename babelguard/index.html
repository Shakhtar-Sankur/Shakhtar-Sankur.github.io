02 · How it works

Clean. Extract. Link.

Every message passes three stages on arrival. A campaign that starts in Hindi is recognised when it reappears in Odia or English, from its first few reports.

STAGE 1

Strip the disguise

Removes the invisible characters and look-alike letters scammers use to slip past filters, while keeping the joiners that Hindi, Bengali, Odia, Urdu and Thai need. Native digits (०–९, ୦–୯, ٠–٩) become 0–9. Every removal is counted: a disguise is evidence.

STAGE 2

Find what the scam needs

Links, phone numbers in any digits, UPI IDs, emails and crypto wallets, checked by checksum. Victims' OTPs, card, Aadhaar and PAN numbers are masked before anything is shared.

STAGE 3

Link across languages

Messages join one campaign when they share an indicator, are near copies, or say the same thing in another language.

04 · What Babelguard adds

Built for the rest of the world

Built for the languages that today's defences serve least, and published with every number reproducible.

  1. A map of the gap

    Where open scam data comes from, language by language, and how much protection an English-built method loses in each, with confidence intervals and the small samples marked rather than ranked.

  2. Script-aware defence

    A cleaner that removes disguises without breaking the scripts that need joiners, and an indicator extractor that reads phone numbers in native digits, UPI IDs and wallets, with victims' details masked.

  3. Linking by meaning

    Cross-language recognition from 1.5% to 85%, so one fact-check or takedown can cover every language version of a campaign.

  4. An engine that holds up

    Streaming, crash-safe linking at 4,057 messages a second on four cores, with 50 crashes and nothing lost: built to run on a CERT's own machines, so no report leaves them.

05 · Press & partners

The story in five facts

Free to quote with credit to “Sankur Kundu, Babelguard”. Every figure links back to code and data.

  • In the largest open set of reported scam SMS, 65% are English; South Asian languages are 0.8% and African languages 0.1%.
  • An English keyword filter catches 86% of English scams but only 10–36% in Korean, Japanese, Urdu, Arabic, Thai and Hindi.
  • Hindi has 175 reported messages in the open data; Tamil, Telugu, Odia and Marathi have none.
  • Text matching recognises a translated scam 1.5% of the time; Babelguard's meaning link, 85%.
  • The engine links 4,057 messages a second on one four-core machine and lost nothing in 50 crashes.
